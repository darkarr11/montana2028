A BILL

To liberate the people of Montana from income and property taxation by creating a transparent, self-adjusting fiscal system funded through natural resource royalties and a voluntary consumption tax on non-essential new goods and services, with surplus revenues returned directly to Montana residents, full public accountability for every dollar collected and spent, and all rates, thresholds, and exemptions determined by the Legislature.

Be it enacted by the Legislature of the State of Montana:

Summary — What This Bill Does

The Montana Flux Tax Act replaces income and property taxes with two revenue sources: royalties on Montana’s natural resources and a consumption tax on non-essential new goods and services. The rates fluctuate based on need — if there’s a surplus, rates go down; if there’s a shortfall, rates go up. Every dollar is tracked in real time on a public ledger. The Legislature decides every number.

No More Income Tax

Montana eliminates the state income tax. Your paycheck is yours. Every dollar you earn stays in your pocket. No withholdings, no filings, no audits. The state funds itself without taxing your labor.

No More Property Tax

Montana phases out the property tax on a timeline set by the Legislature. When you own your home, you own it. No more paying rent to the government on something you already bought. Retirees on fixed incomes stop living in fear of rising assessments.

Montana’s Resources Fund Montana

Mining, oil and gas, timber, coal, wind, solar, water — Montana’s natural resources generate royalty revenue for the people who own them: Montanans. The Legislature sets the rates per resource. Companies pay for what they extract. The money funds the state.

Consumption Tax on New Non-Essentials

A consumption tax applies only to new, non-essential goods and services. Groceries, medicine, housing, utilities, childcare — exempt. Used and secondhand goods — never taxed. You choose what to buy. You choose when to pay. That’s the voluntary part.

The Montana Resource Dividend

When resource revenues exceed what the state needs, the surplus goes back to the people. Every eligible Montana resident receives an annual dividend payment. Alaska does it with oil. Montana does it with everything.

The Flux: Rates That Adjust

If the state collects more than it needs, the consumption tax rate drops automatically. If revenues fall short, the rate rises. The system self-corrects. No emergency sessions. No budget crises. No political games. The math does the work.

Every Dollar Visible

All revenue and all spending published in real time on a public ledger. Every royalty payment, every consumption tax dollar, every dividend distribution — visible to every Montanan. No hidden funds. No slush accounts. Total transparency.

Used Goods Are Never Taxed

Buy a used truck, a secondhand couch, a refurbished laptop — zero tax. This promotes recycling, sustainability, and affordability. The tax only applies when something is manufactured and sold new. After that first sale, it’s free and clear forever.

Montana Stops Renting Its Future from Washington

A fiscal independence strategy to reduce reliance on conditional federal grants that come with mandates attached. Montana’s money stays in Montana. Montana’s decisions stay in Montana. The Legislature controls the pace.

The Legislature Decides Everything

This bill creates the framework — the committees, the processes, the mechanisms, the timeline. It does not prescribe rates, percentages, dollar amounts, or specific exemptions. Those are for the Legislature and the people of Montana to determine. This is the beginning of a conversation, not the end of one.

The bottom line: Your labor belongs to you. Your home belongs to you. Montana’s resources belong to Montanans. This bill builds the system that makes all three of those statements true.

SECTION 2. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE

(a) FINDINGS

The Legislature of the State of Montana finds the following:

  1. The current tax system in Montana — and in the United States generally — is a relic of the twentieth century that punishes productivity, penalizes homeownership, invades personal privacy, and forces citizens to navigate a labyrinth of rules, filings, and bureaucratic compliance that costs billions of dollars annually and serves no one except the compliance industry itself.
  2. Montana’s state income tax requires every working Montanan to disclose intimate details of their financial lives to the government, submit to the threat of audits, and surrender a portion of their labor before they ever see it. This system treats earned income as a government resource to be allocated, rather than a private asset to be respected.
  3. Montana’s property tax creates a condition in which no Montanan ever truly owns their home. A family that has paid off its mortgage in full remains subject to annual tax assessments that function as perpetual rent to the government. Retirees on fixed incomes face rising assessments that can force them from homes they have occupied for decades. The concept of homeownership is an illusion when the government can take your property for failure to pay an annual fee on something you already own.
  4. Montana sits atop extraordinary natural resource wealth — copper, gold, silver, sapphires, talc, oil, natural gas, coal, timber, wind, solar energy, and water — that belongs to the people of Montana. The extraction and commercial use of these resources should fund the operations of the state government that serves those people, rather than requiring the taxation of individual labor and private property.
  5. The State of Alaska has demonstrated for more than forty years that a resource-based revenue model can fund state government and return surplus revenue directly to residents through an annual dividend, proving that this approach is neither theoretical nor untested.
  6. A consumption tax on non-essential new goods and services aligns taxation with individual choice. Citizens who consume more contribute more. Citizens who consume less contribute less. Used and secondhand goods, having already been taxed at first sale, are never taxed again — promoting sustainability, recycling, and affordability.
  7. Essential goods and services — including but not limited to groceries, medicine, healthcare, housing, utilities, and childcare — should never be subject to consumption tax, ensuring that the system does not burden those least able to bear it.
  8. A dynamic rate adjustment mechanism — the “flux” — that automatically raises or lowers the consumption tax rate based on revenue versus budgetary need ensures fiscal stability without emergency legislative sessions, political brinksmanship, or budget crises.
  9. Full transparency in government revenue and spending — every dollar collected, every dollar spent, visible in real time to every citizen — is not a luxury but a requirement of legitimate governance. A government that hides its finances is a government that serves itself.
  10. Montana’s current dependence on conditional federal funding compromises state sovereignty by requiring compliance with federal mandates as a condition of receiving Montana’s own tax dollars back. A fiscally independent Montana makes its own decisions without permission from Washington.
  11. The specific rates, thresholds, exemptions, and implementation timelines for this system should be determined by the Legislature through deliberative process, informed by economic analysis, public input, and the unique needs of Montana’s communities. This Act creates the framework. The Legislature fills in the numbers. That is how representative government is supposed to work.

(b) PURPOSE

The purpose of this Act is to—

  1. Eliminate the Montana state income tax, restoring to every working Montanan the full value of their labor;
  2. Phase out the Montana property tax, ensuring that homeownership means actual ownership;
  3. Establish a Montana Natural Resource Royalty System that generates state revenue from the commercial extraction and use of Montana’s natural resources;
  4. Establish a consumption tax on non-essential new goods and services as a supplementary revenue source, with essential goods and used goods permanently exempt;
  5. Create the Montana Resource Dividend, returning surplus resource revenues directly to Montana residents;
  6. Implement a Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism that automatically adjusts consumption tax rates based on revenue performance relative to budgetary need;
  7. Require full, real-time public transparency of all state revenue and expenditures;
  8. Begin the process of reducing Montana’s dependence on conditional federal funding;
  9. Establish a standing Legislative Oversight Committee to review, adjust, and refine the system on an ongoing basis; and
  10. Prove that a state can fund its government without taxing the labor or property of its people.

SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS

In this Act:

(1) FLUX RATE

The term “Flux Rate” means the consumption tax rate in effect during any given adjustment period, as calculated under the Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism established in Section 9 of this Act. The Flux Rate may increase or decrease based on the relationship between actual revenue and budgetary need.

(2) RESOURCE ROYALTY

The term “Resource Royalty” means the fee imposed on any person or entity engaged in the commercial extraction, harvesting, generation, or use of Montana’s natural resources, as established under Section 6 of this Act.

(3) ESSENTIAL GOOD OR SERVICE

The term “Essential Good or Service” means any good or service designated by the Legislature as exempt from the consumption tax under Section 7 of this Act, on the basis that the good or service is necessary for basic human welfare. The Legislature shall establish and maintain the list of Essential Goods and Services, which shall include, at minimum, categories relating to food, medicine, healthcare, housing, utilities, childcare, and education.

(4) NON-ESSENTIAL NEW GOOD OR SERVICE

The term “Non-Essential New Good or Service” means any good or service that is—(A) sold for the first time at retail in Montana; and(B) not designated as an Essential Good or Service under Section 7.

(5) USED OR SECONDHAND GOOD

The term “Used or Secondhand Good” means any tangible good that has been previously sold at retail and is being resold, regardless of condition, refurbishment, or modification. Used or Secondhand Goods are permanently exempt from the consumption tax under this Act.

(6) CONSUMPTION TAX

The term “Consumption Tax” means the tax imposed under Section 7 of this Act on the retail sale of Non-Essential New Goods and Services in Montana, at the Flux Rate in effect at the time of sale.

(7) MONTANA RESOURCE DIVIDEND

The term “Montana Resource Dividend” means the annual payment to eligible Montana residents from surplus Resource Royalty revenues, as established under Section 8 of this Act.

(8) MONTANA RESOURCE REVENUE FUND

The term “Montana Resource Revenue Fund” means the dedicated state fund established under Section 6 of this Act into which all Resource Royalty revenues are deposited and from which state operations, the Resource Dividend, and reserve allocations are funded.

(9) ADJUSTMENT PERIOD

The term “Adjustment Period” means the regular interval — as determined by the Legislature, which may be monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, or annual — at which the Flux Rate is recalculated under the Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism.

(10) COVERED NATURAL RESOURCE

The term “Covered Natural Resource” means any natural resource subject to Resource Royalty under Section 6 of this Act, including but not limited to minerals, metals, gemstones, oil, natural gas, coal, timber, wind energy, solar energy, water resources, and any additional resource designated by the Legislature.

(11) LEGISLATIVE OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE

The term “Legislative Oversight Committee” means the standing joint committee of the Montana Legislature established under Section 13 of this Act to oversee, review, and recommend adjustments to all components of the fiscal system created by this Act.

(12) ELIGIBLE RESIDENT

The term “Eligible Resident” means any individual who qualifies for the Montana Resource Dividend under the criteria established by the Legislature in Section 8, which shall include, at minimum, a requirement of bona fide Montana residency for a period determined by the Legislature.

(13) PUBLIC REVENUE LEDGER

The term “Public Revenue Ledger” means the real-time, publicly accessible financial transparency system established under Section 10 of this Act, through which all revenues collected and expenditures made under this Act are published and made available to the public.

SECTION 4. ELIMINATION OF STATE INCOME TAX

(a) REPEAL

Upon full implementation of this Act, as determined by the transition timeline established in Section 11, the Montana state individual income tax imposed under Title 15, Chapter 30, Montana Code Annotated, shall be repealed in its entirety.

(b) SCOPE OF REPEAL

The repeal under this section shall include—

  1. All individual income tax obligations, including withholding requirements;
  2. All filing requirements associated with the state individual income tax;
  3. All penalties, interest, and enforcement mechanisms associated with the state individual income tax; and
  4. All state tax credits, deductions, and exemptions that exist solely in relation to the state individual income tax.

(c) CORPORATE AND BUSINESS INCOME TAX

The Legislature shall determine whether and on what timeline to repeal or modify the Montana corporate income tax and other business income taxes in coordination with the implementation of the Resource Royalty and Consumption Tax systems established by this Act. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall study the fiscal impact of corporate income tax repeal and report its findings and recommendations to the Legislature within the timeframe established in Section 11.

(d) TRANSITION PROTECTION

  1. During the transition period established in Section 11, the income tax shall be reduced in phases as Resource Royalty and Consumption Tax revenues demonstrate sufficient capacity to replace income tax revenue.
  2. No Montanan shall be subject to both the full income tax rate and the full Consumption Tax simultaneously. As the Consumption Tax is phased in, the income tax shall be phased out by corresponding amounts, ensuring that the total tax burden on Montanans does not increase during the transition.
  3. The Legislature shall establish the specific phase-out schedule, reduction percentages, and trigger points for each phase of the transition.

(e) EFFECT ON FEDERAL TAXES

Nothing in this Act affects the obligation of Montana residents to pay federal income taxes. However, the Legislature finds that the elimination of state income tax will increase the take-home pay of every working Montanan and reduce the compliance burden on individuals and businesses. The Legislature further encourages the United States Congress to consider similar reforms at the federal level.

(f) SAVINGS TO MONTANANS

The elimination of the state income tax will—

  1. Eliminate the cost of state income tax preparation for every individual and business in Montana;
  2. Eliminate the privacy intrusion of annual financial disclosure to the state;
  3. Eliminate the risk of state income tax audits;
  4. Return to every working Montanan the full value of their labor; and
  5. Make Montana one of the most attractive states in the nation for workers, entrepreneurs, and businesses.

SECTION 5. ELIMINATION OF PROPERTY TAX

(a) PHASE-OUT

The Montana property tax, as imposed under Title 15, Chapters 6 through 10, Montana Code Annotated, shall be phased out and ultimately repealed on a timeline and schedule determined by the Legislature, in coordination with the transition plan established in Section 11.

(b) LEGISLATIVE DETERMINATION

The Legislature shall determine—

  1. The duration of the phase-out period;
  2. The annual or periodic reduction percentages;
  3. Whether the phase-out shall apply uniformly to all property types or be sequenced by category (residential, agricultural, commercial, industrial);
  4. The trigger points at which each phase of the reduction takes effect, based on demonstrated replacement revenue from Resource Royalties and the Consumption Tax; and
  5. Any interim protections for property owners during the transition, including caps on assessment increases.

(c) LOCAL GOVERNMENT REVENUE REPLACEMENT

  1. Property tax revenue currently funds a significant portion of county and municipal operations in Montana, including schools, roads, law enforcement, and local services. The elimination of property tax shall not result in the elimination of these services.
  2. The Legislature shall establish a Local Government Revenue Replacement mechanism that directs a portion of Resource Royalty and Consumption Tax revenues to counties and municipalities to replace property tax revenue.
  3. The allocation formula shall be determined by the Legislature and shall account for differences in local government size, population, geographic area, infrastructure needs, and current property tax dependence.
  4. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall monitor local government fiscal health throughout the transition and recommend adjustments as necessary.

(d) HOMESTEAD PROTECTION

  1. During the phase-out period, the Legislature shall prioritize the reduction of property taxes on primary residences occupied by the homeowner.
  2. The Legislature may establish a Homestead Exemption that accelerates the property tax phase-out for primary residences, particularly for—
      1. retirees and individuals on fixed incomes;
      1. veterans;
      1. individuals with disabilities; and
      1. families below a household income threshold determined by the Legislature.
  3. No Montanan shall lose their home due to inability to pay property taxes during the transition period. The Legislature shall establish protections to prevent tax-sale foreclosures on primary residences during the phase-out.

(e) AGRICULTURAL LAND

The Legislature shall give special consideration to the treatment of agricultural land during the property tax phase-out, recognizing that Montana’s agricultural economy depends on the ability of ranchers and farmers to hold and work land without the burden of property taxation that bears no relationship to the productive capacity of the land in any given year.

(f) UPON FULL REPEAL

Upon full repeal of the property tax—

  1. No Montana resident or business shall owe any property tax to any state, county, or municipal government in Montana;
  2. All property tax lien authority shall be terminated;
  3. All property tax assessment and collection infrastructure shall be wound down; and
  4. Homeownership in Montana shall mean what the word says: you own it.

SECTION 6. MONTANA NATURAL RESOURCE ROYALTY SYSTEM

(a) ESTABLISHMENT

There is hereby established the Montana Natural Resource Royalty System, through which the State of Montana shall collect royalties from any person or entity engaged in the commercial extraction, harvesting, generation, or use of Covered Natural Resources within the borders of Montana.

(b) COVERED NATURAL RESOURCES

The following categories of natural resources shall be subject to Resource Royalty under this Act, with the Legislature establishing specific royalty rates, measurement methods, and collection procedures for each:

  1. MINERALS AND METALS. Including but not limited to copper, gold, silver, platinum, palladium, and any other metallic mineral extracted from Montana lands or waters;
  2. GEMSTONES. Including but not limited to sapphires, garnets, agates, and any other gemstone extracted from Montana lands or waters;
  3. INDUSTRIAL MINERALS. Including but not limited to talc, vermiculite, bentonite, limestone, and sand and gravel extracted for commercial use;
  4. OIL AND NATURAL GAS. Including all crude oil, natural gas, condensate, and associated hydrocarbons extracted from Montana lands or waters;
  5. COAL. Including all grades of coal extracted from Montana lands;
  6. TIMBER. Including all commercial timber harvested from Montana lands, with the Legislature establishing separate rates for state lands, federal lands within Montana where the state collects fees, and private lands;
  7. WIND ENERGY. Including all commercial wind energy generated by wind farms and commercial wind installations in Montana;
  8. SOLAR ENERGY. Including all commercial solar energy generated by solar farms and commercial solar installations in Montana;
  9. WATER RESOURCES. Including commercial water extraction, bottling, diversion for industrial use, and any other commercial use of Montana’s surface or groundwater resources beyond individual domestic and agricultural use; and
  10. ADDITIONAL RESOURCES. The Legislature may designate additional natural resources as Covered Natural Resources by statute at any time.

(c) ROYALTY RATE DETERMINATION

  1. The Legislature shall establish the royalty rate for each category of Covered Natural Resource. Rates may be expressed as a percentage of gross value, a per-unit fee, or any other method the Legislature determines to be fair and administrable.
  2. Royalty rates may differ by resource category, reflecting differences in extraction cost, environmental impact, market value, and the strategic importance of each resource to Montana’s economy.
  3. The Legislature shall review and may adjust royalty rates at least once every biennium, informed by recommendations from the Legislative Oversight Committee.
  4. In setting royalty rates, the Legislature shall balance the following objectives—
      1. generating sufficient revenue to fund state operations and the Montana Resource Dividend;
      1. maintaining Montana’s competitiveness as a location for resource development;
      1. ensuring that the people of Montana receive fair value for their natural resources; and
      1. encouraging responsible extraction practices that protect Montana’s environment and natural heritage.

(d) MONTANA RESOURCE REVENUE FUND

  1. All Resource Royalty revenues shall be deposited into the Montana Resource Revenue Fund, which is hereby established in the Montana state treasury.
  2. The Montana Resource Revenue Fund shall be the primary funding source for state government operations under this Act.
  3. The Fund shall be divided into three components, with the Legislature determining the allocation percentages—
      1. Operations. The portion allocated to fund state government operations, including all departments, agencies, programs, and services;
      1. Reserve. The portion allocated to a permanent reserve fund that provides fiscal stability during periods of low resource revenue. The reserve shall accumulate until it reaches a cap determined by the Legislature, after which reserve allocations shall be redirected to Operations or the Dividend; and
      1. Dividend. The portion allocated to fund the Montana Resource Dividend established in Section 8.

(e) COLLECTION AND ENFORCEMENT

  1. The Montana Department of Revenue shall administer the collection of Resource Royalties.
  2. All entities subject to Resource Royalty shall file reports and remit payments on a schedule determined by the Legislature.
  3. Failure to pay Resource Royalties shall be subject to penalties established by the Legislature, including interest on unpaid amounts, civil penalties, and suspension or revocation of operating permits.
  4. Willful evasion of Resource Royalties shall be a criminal offense, with penalties established by the Legislature.

(f) ENVIRONMENTAL ACCOUNTABILITY

Nothing in this Act shall be construed to reduce, eliminate, or weaken any existing environmental protection, reclamation requirement, bonding obligation, or cleanup liability applicable to natural resource extraction in Montana. Resource Royalties are a payment for the value of the resource extracted. Environmental compliance is a separate obligation. Both are required.

SECTION 7. CONSUMPTION TAX ON NON-ESSENTIAL NEW GOODS AND SERVICES

(a) IMPOSITION

There is hereby imposed a consumption tax on the retail sale of every Non-Essential New Good and Service sold in Montana, at the Flux Rate in effect at the time of sale, as determined under the Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism established in Section 9.

(b) WHO PAYS

The consumption tax shall be paid by the purchaser at the point of sale. The seller shall collect the tax and remit it to the Montana Department of Revenue on a schedule determined by the Legislature.

(c) ESSENTIAL GOODS AND SERVICES — PERMANENTLY EXEMPT

  1. The Legislature shall establish and maintain a comprehensive list of Essential Goods and Services that are permanently exempt from the consumption tax.
  2. The list shall include, at minimum, the following categories—
      1. Food and groceries. All food purchased for home consumption, including raw ingredients, packaged food, dairy, meat, produce, bread, and non-alcoholic beverages;
      1. Medicine and healthcare. All prescription medications, over-the-counter medicines, medical devices, healthcare services, health insurance premiums, dental care, vision care, and mental health services;
      1. Housing. Rent, mortgage payments, and home repair and maintenance services necessary to maintain a primary residence;
      1. Utilities. Electricity, natural gas, heating fuel, water, sewer, and basic telecommunications services including internet access;
      1. Childcare and education. Daycare, preschool, K-12 education expenses, tutoring, educational materials, and post-secondary tuition; and
      1. Additional categories. Any additional goods or services the Legislature determines to be essential for basic human welfare.
  3. The Legislature may add to or modify the Essential Goods and Services list by statute at any time. No item shall be removed from the list without a supermajority vote of both chambers, as determined by the Legislature.

(d) USED AND SECONDHAND GOODS — PERMANENTLY EXEMPT

  1. No consumption tax shall be imposed on the sale of any Used or Secondhand Good, regardless of the value, condition, or nature of the good.
  2. This exemption is absolute and permanent. A good that has been sold at retail once and had the consumption tax collected at that first sale shall never be subject to the consumption tax again, regardless of how many times it is subsequently resold.
  3. This provision promotes recycling, sustainability, and affordability, and ensures that the consumption tax does not function as a cascading tax that burdens goods repeatedly.

(e) INITIAL FLUX RATE

The Legislature shall establish the initial Flux Rate for the consumption tax. The initial rate shall be informed by economic analysis prepared by the Legislative Oversight Committee and shall be set at a level that, combined with projected Resource Royalty revenues, is sufficient to fund state operations and the transition from income and property taxes without increasing the overall tax burden on Montanans.

(f) COLLECTION INFRASTRUCTURE

  1. The Montana Department of Revenue shall establish the infrastructure necessary to collect the consumption tax, including point-of-sale integration requirements for Montana retailers.
  2. The Department shall provide technical assistance to small businesses and rural retailers to ensure compliance without undue burden.
  3. The Legislature shall establish a reasonable transition period for retailers to implement collection systems, during which the Department shall provide support and waive penalties for good-faith compliance efforts.

(g) BORDER AND REMOTE TRANSACTIONS

  1. The Legislature shall establish provisions for the collection of consumption tax on—
      1. online and remote sales delivered to Montana addresses;
      1. purchases made by Montana residents in border communities; and
      1. transactions involving out-of-state sellers.
  2. The provisions shall be designed to ensure fair collection without creating an undue burden on interstate commerce, consistent with applicable federal law.

(h) LOCAL OPTION

The Legislature shall determine whether counties and municipalities may impose an additional local consumption tax to replace property tax revenue during and after the transition, and if so, the maximum rate and conditions under which local consumption taxes may be imposed.

SECTION 8. THE MONTANA RESOURCE DIVIDEND

(a) ESTABLISHMENT

There is hereby established the Montana Resource Dividend — an annual payment to every Eligible Resident of Montana, funded from the Dividend portion of the Montana Resource Revenue Fund established in Section 6.

(b) PRINCIPLE

Montana’s natural resources belong to the people of Montana. When the commercial extraction and use of those resources generates revenue beyond what is needed to fund state operations and maintain fiscal reserves, the surplus shall be returned to the owners: the people.

(c) ELIGIBILITY

  1. The Legislature shall establish eligibility criteria for the Montana Resource Dividend, which shall include, at minimum—
      1. bona fide Montana residency for a continuous period determined by the Legislature;
      1. physical presence in Montana for a minimum number of days per year, as determined by the Legislature; and
      1. filing of a simple annual dividend application with the Department of Revenue.
  2. The Legislature shall determine whether eligibility extends to all ages or whether minors receive a reduced dividend or a dividend held in trust.
  3. The Legislature shall establish provisions to prevent fraud, including identity verification and residency confirmation.

(d) DIVIDEND CALCULATION

  1. The annual Dividend amount shall be determined by dividing the Dividend allocation of the Montana Resource Revenue Fund by the number of Eligible Residents.
  2. The Dividend amount will vary from year to year based on resource revenue performance. In years of high resource revenue, the Dividend will be larger. In years of lower revenue, it will be smaller. In years where resource revenue is insufficient to fund both operations and the Dividend, the Dividend may be reduced or suspended.
  3. State operations and the fiscal reserve shall always be funded before the Dividend is calculated. The Dividend is the surplus, not the floor.

(e) PAYMENT

  1. The Montana Resource Dividend shall be paid annually, on a date determined by the Legislature.
  2. Payment shall be made by direct deposit, check, or other method established by the Department of Revenue.
  3. The Dividend shall not be subject to state income tax (which will have been eliminated) or any other state withholding. It is a return of resource wealth to its owners.

(f) NOT AN ENTITLEMENT

The Montana Resource Dividend is not a welfare program, a social benefit, or an entitlement. It is the people’s share of the commercial value of resources they collectively own. The Alaska Permanent Fund has operated on this principle since 1982. Montana adapts and improves the model by applying it to the full range of Montana’s natural resource wealth, not just oil.

(g) DIVIDEND INVESTMENT

  1. The Legislature may establish a permanent investment fund, similar to the Alaska Permanent Fund, into which a portion of Resource Royalty revenues is deposited and invested for long-term growth.
  2. Investment earnings from the permanent fund may supplement or stabilize the annual Dividend, smoothing fluctuations in resource revenue.
  3. The principal of any permanent fund established under this section shall not be appropriated for any purpose other than investment. Only earnings may be distributed.
  4. The structure, governance, and investment policies of any such fund shall be determined by the Legislature.

SECTION 9. DYNAMIC RATE ADJUSTMENT MECHANISM

(a) THE FLUX

The Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism — the “flux” — is the self-correcting system by which the consumption tax rate automatically adjusts based on the relationship between actual state revenue and budgetary need. This is the core innovation of the Flux Tax: a tax system that responds to reality rather than requiring political intervention every time revenue projections miss.

(b) HOW IT WORKS

  1. At the end of each Adjustment Period, the Department of Revenue shall calculate—
      1. total actual revenue received from all sources (Resource Royalties, Consumption Tax, and any other revenue); and
      1. total budgeted expenditures for the current fiscal period, as appropriated by the Legislature.
  2. SURPLUS. If actual revenue exceeds budgeted expenditures by more than a threshold percentage determined by the Legislature—
      1. the Flux Rate shall be reduced for the next Adjustment Period by an amount calculated under the formula established by the Legislature; and
      1. excess revenues shall be allocated to the fiscal reserve and the Montana Resource Dividend, in proportions determined by the Legislature.
  3. SHORTFALL. If actual revenue falls below budgeted expenditures by more than a threshold percentage determined by the Legislature—
      1. the Flux Rate shall be increased for the next Adjustment Period by an amount calculated under the formula established by the Legislature; and
      1. if the fiscal reserve contains sufficient funds, the reserve shall be drawn upon before the Flux Rate is increased, to the extent determined by the Legislature.
  4. EQUILIBRIUM. If actual revenue falls within the threshold band around budgeted expenditures, the Flux Rate shall remain unchanged.

(c) RATE BOUNDARIES

  1. The Legislature shall establish a floor and a ceiling for the Flux Rate. The rate shall never fall below the floor and never rise above the ceiling, regardless of the automatic adjustment calculation.
  2. If the automatic adjustment would push the rate beyond the ceiling, the Legislative Oversight Committee shall convene within 30 days to recommend emergency measures to the Legislature.
  3. If the automatic adjustment would push the rate below the floor, the excess revenue shall be directed to the fiscal reserve and the Montana Resource Dividend.

(d) ADJUSTMENT PERIOD

The Legislature shall determine the Adjustment Period — the regular interval at which the Flux Rate is recalculated. The Legislature may set the Adjustment Period at monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, or annual intervals, and may change the interval based on experience and economic conditions.

(e) TRANSPARENCY

  1. Every Flux Rate adjustment shall be published on the Public Revenue Ledger at least 30 days before it takes effect.
  2. The calculation underlying each adjustment — including all revenue figures, expenditure figures, and the formula applied — shall be published in full.
  3. Any Montanan shall be able to verify the Flux Rate calculation independently using publicly available data. No black boxes. No discretionary adjustments. The math is public.

(f) OVERRIDE

The Legislature retains the authority to override any automatic Flux Rate adjustment by statute, provided that any override is temporary (not exceeding one fiscal year) and is accompanied by a written explanation published on the Public Revenue Ledger. The override authority exists for genuine emergencies. It is not a tool for political manipulation of the rate.

(g) ANTI-GAMING PROVISIONS

  1. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall monitor for any pattern of deliberate over-budgeting designed to keep the Flux Rate artificially high, or deliberate under-budgeting designed to generate artificial surpluses.
  2. The Committee shall report any such pattern to the Legislature and the public.
  3. The Legislature may establish penalties for executive branch officials who submit knowingly inflated or deflated budget projections for the purpose of manipulating the Flux Rate.

SECTION 10. REVENUE TRANSPARENCY AND PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

(a) THE PUBLIC REVENUE LEDGER

The Department of Revenue, in coordination with the Department of Administration, shall establish and maintain the Public Revenue Ledger — a real-time, publicly accessible platform that displays all revenue collected and all expenditures made under this Act.

(b) SCOPE

The Public Revenue Ledger shall display—

  1. All Resource Royalty collections, broken down by resource category, company or entity, amount, and date;
  2. All Consumption Tax collections, broken down by region, category, amount, and date;
  3. All deposits into and withdrawals from the Montana Resource Revenue Fund, including the Operations, Reserve, and Dividend components;
  4. All Flux Rate adjustments, including the calculation data and formula used;
  5. All Montana Resource Dividend distributions, including total distributed, number of recipients, and per-person amount;
  6. All state government expenditures funded by the Montana Resource Revenue Fund, at the same level of detail required by the Montana Transparency Act if enacted; and
  7. All Local Government Revenue Replacement distributions, by county and municipality.

(c) REAL-TIME PUBLICATION

Data shall be published to the Public Revenue Ledger within 24 hours of the transaction occurring. The Department shall establish automated systems to minimize manual data entry and ensure timely publication.

(d) PUBLIC ACCESS

  1. The Public Revenue Ledger shall be accessible to any person through a public website at no cost.
  2. No account registration or identification shall be required to access the Ledger.
  3. All data shall be available for download in open, machine-readable formats.
  4. If the Montana Transparency Act is enacted, the Public Revenue Ledger shall integrate with the Montana Public Ledger and the Citizen Intelligence System established by that Act.

(e) ANNUAL FISCAL REPORT

  1. The Department of Revenue shall publish an annual fiscal report detailing—
      1. total revenue by source;
      1. total expenditures by category;
      1. the performance of the Flux Rate adjustment mechanism;
      1. the status of the fiscal reserve;
      1. the Montana Resource Dividend distribution; and
      1. a comparison of projected versus actual revenue and expenditures.
  2. The annual report shall be submitted to the Legislature and published on the Public Revenue Ledger.

(f) CITIZEN AUDIT

Any Montana citizen may submit a written request for an explanation of any transaction or data point on the Public Revenue Ledger. The Department shall respond within 30 days. If the Montana Transparency Act is enacted, citizens may query the Citizen Intelligence System for instant answers. The point is simple: every dollar that comes in and every dollar that goes out is visible to the people who own both the resources and the government.

SECTION 11. TRANSITION TIMELINE AND IMPLEMENTATION

(a) PHASED APPROACH

The transition from Montana’s current tax system to the Flux Tax system shall be phased, deliberate, and responsive to real-world data. The Legislature controls the pace. Nothing moves forward until the evidence demonstrates it is safe to do so.

(b) PHASE ONE — ESTABLISHMENT

Within the timeframe established by the Legislature following the effective date of this Act—

  1. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall be formed and begin operations;
  2. The Department of Revenue shall begin establishing the infrastructure for Resource Royalty collection and Consumption Tax collection;
  3. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall commission economic analyses and fiscal modeling to inform the Legislature’s determination of initial royalty rates, the initial Flux Rate, Essential Goods exemptions, and other key parameters;
  4. The Public Revenue Ledger shall be designed and prepared for launch; and
  5. A public education campaign shall begin to inform Montana citizens and businesses about the upcoming changes.

(c) PHASE TWO — PILOT AND INITIAL IMPLEMENTATION

Following Phase One, on a timeline determined by the Legislature—

  1. The Legislature shall enact the initial royalty rates, the initial Flux Rate, the Essential Goods and Services list, and the eligibility criteria for the Montana Resource Dividend;
  2. Resource Royalty collection shall begin;
  3. The Consumption Tax shall be implemented, initially at a rate that may be lower than the full projected rate to allow for adjustment;
  4. The Public Revenue Ledger shall launch;
  5. The first phase of income tax reduction shall take effect, in an amount corresponding to new revenue demonstrated; and
  6. The Legislature may designate pilot regions or communities to implement the property tax phase-out ahead of the statewide schedule.

(d) PHASE THREE — EXPANSION AND ADJUSTMENT

Following Phase Two, on a timeline determined by the Legislature—

  1. The income tax shall continue its phased reduction based on demonstrated replacement revenue;
  2. The property tax phase-out shall expand statewide on the schedule established by the Legislature;
  3. The Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism shall become fully operational;
  4. The first Montana Resource Dividend shall be calculated and distributed; and
  5. The Legislative Oversight Committee shall submit its first comprehensive review and adjustment recommendations.

(e) PHASE FOUR — FULL IMPLEMENTATION

On a timeline determined by the Legislature—

  1. The state income tax shall be fully repealed;
  2. The property tax shall be fully repealed;
  3. The Flux Tax system shall be the sole source of state revenue; and
  4. Montana shall be the first state in the nation to fund its government entirely without taxing the labor or property of its people.

(f) LEGISLATIVE CONTROL

  1. The Legislature may accelerate, slow, pause, or modify any phase of the transition based on fiscal performance, economic conditions, or public input.
  2. No phase shall advance automatically. Each phase requires affirmative legislative action based on evidence.
  3. If at any point the data demonstrates that the new revenue sources are insufficient to maintain state services, the Legislature shall have full authority to adjust rates, modify timelines, or take any other action necessary to protect Montana’s fiscal health.

(g) NO DISRUPTION OF SERVICES

Throughout the transition, state government services shall continue without interruption. No school shall close. No road shall go unpaved. No law enforcement agency shall lose funding. The transition replaces how Montana funds its government, not whether Montana funds its government.

SECTION 12. MONTANA FISCAL INDEPENDENCE

(a) FINDINGS

The Legislature finds that Montana currently receives federal funding that comes with conditions, mandates, reporting requirements, and policy constraints that limit Montana’s ability to govern itself according to the values and priorities of its own people. Federal funding is not free money — it is Montana’s own tax dollars sent to Washington, skimmed, and returned with strings attached.

(b) STRATEGY

The Legislature shall develop and implement a long-term strategy to reduce Montana’s dependence on conditional federal grants and funding, with the goal of increasing Montana’s fiscal sovereignty and decision-making independence.

(c) PRINCIPLES

The fiscal independence strategy shall be guided by the following principles—

  1. Montana should not accept federal funding that requires Montana to adopt policies, programs, or mandates that the Legislature or the people of Montana have not independently chosen;
  2. Where federal funding can be replaced by state revenue from the Resource Royalty and Consumption Tax systems without reducing services to Montanans, Montana should replace it;
  3. Where federal funding cannot yet be replaced, Montana should negotiate for the removal of conditional mandates while retaining the funding; and
  4. Montana should advocate at the federal level for the reduction of Montana’s federal tax burden by an amount corresponding to declined federal funding, so that Montana’s taxpayers are not subsidizing other states’ programs while receiving nothing in return.

(d) ASSESSMENT

  1. Within the timeframe established by the Legislature, the Legislative Oversight Committee shall produce a comprehensive assessment of all federal funding currently received by Montana, including—
      1. the total dollar amount by program;
      1. the conditions, mandates, and reporting requirements attached to each funding source;
      1. the estimated cost of compliance with those conditions;
      1. the feasibility and timeline for replacing each funding source with state revenue; and
      1. the risks and benefits of declining each funding source.
  2. The assessment shall be published on the Public Revenue Ledger and presented to the Legislature.

(e) PHASED APPROACH

The fiscal independence strategy shall be implemented gradually, with the Legislature determining the sequence and timeline based on the assessment described in subsection (d). The Legislature shall prioritize declining federal funding that carries the most burdensome conditions relative to the funding amount.

(f) PROTECTION OF ESSENTIAL SERVICES

Nothing in this section shall be construed to require Montana to decline federal funding in a manner that would result in the loss of essential services for Montana citizens, including healthcare, education, infrastructure, and public safety, until replacement revenue is demonstrated to be sufficient and sustainable.

(g) INVITATION TO OTHER STATES

The Legislature encourages other states to develop similar fiscal independence strategies. When multiple states reduce their dependence on conditional federal funding simultaneously, the federal government’s ability to use funding as a tool of coercion diminishes, and the principle of federalism is strengthened.

SECTION 13. LEGISLATIVE OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE

(a) ESTABLISHMENT

There is hereby established a joint standing committee of the Montana Legislature, to be known as the Flux Tax Legislative Oversight Committee.

(b) COMPOSITION

  1. The Committee shall consist of members of both chambers of the Legislature, with the number, selection method, and party balance determined by the rules of the Legislature.
  2. The Committee shall be bipartisan. No single party shall hold more than a simple majority of Committee seats.
  3. The Committee shall select its own chair and vice chair, who shall not be of the same party.

(c) DUTIES

The Flux Tax Legislative Oversight Committee shall—

  1. Monitor the performance of the Resource Royalty system, the Consumption Tax, the Dynamic Rate Adjustment Mechanism, and the Montana Resource Dividend on an ongoing basis;
  2. Commission and review economic analyses, fiscal projections, and impact studies as needed;
  3. Review the Essential Goods and Services list and recommend additions or modifications;
  4. Review Resource Royalty rates and recommend adjustments to the Legislature;
  5. Monitor the transition timeline and recommend whether to advance, slow, or modify each phase;
  6. Monitor the fiscal health of local governments during the property tax phase-out and recommend adjustments to the Revenue Replacement mechanism;
  7. Monitor the fiscal independence strategy and report on progress;
  8. Monitor the Public Revenue Ledger for accuracy and completeness;
  9. Conduct or commission an annual audit of the Montana Resource Revenue Fund;
  10. Receive and investigate complaints from citizens, businesses, or local governments regarding any component of the Flux Tax system;
  11. Hold public hearings at least twice per year, in locations across Montana, to receive public input on the performance and direction of the system; and
  12. Submit an annual report to the full Legislature with findings, recommendations, and proposed legislation.

(d) STAFF

The Committee shall be supported by professional, nonpartisan staff, including at minimum—

  1. A fiscal analyst with expertise in state and local government finance;
  2. A natural resource economist;
  3. An attorney specializing in tax law; and
  4. Administrative support.

Staff shall be hired by the Committee and shall serve the Committee, not any individual member or party.

(e) AUTHORITY

  1. The Committee shall have the authority to subpoena documents and testimony from any state department or agency related to the administration of this Act.
  2. The Committee shall have access to all data on the Public Revenue Ledger, including pre-publication data for audit purposes.
  3. The Committee’s recommendations are advisory. The full Legislature retains all legislative authority. The Committee identifies what needs attention. The Legislature decides what to do about it.

(f) PUBLIC PROCEEDINGS

All Committee meetings, hearings, deliberations, and reports shall be open to the public and published on the Public Revenue Ledger. This Committee works for the people of Montana. The people get to watch.

SECTION 14. SEVERABILITY

If any provision of this Act, or the application of such provision to any person or circumstance, is held invalid by any court of competent jurisdiction, the remainder of this Act, and the application of such provision to other persons or circumstances, shall not be affected thereby.

The provisions of this Act are severable by design. The elimination of the income tax can proceed independently of the property tax phase-out. The Resource Royalty system can operate independently of the Consumption Tax. The Montana Resource Dividend can be implemented at any point where surplus revenues exist. If a court strikes any single component, the remaining components continue to function. The system is modular because the Legislature should be able to implement what works and revisit what doesn’t.

SECTION 15. EFFECTIVE DATE

(a) GENERAL

This Act shall take effect upon passage and approval by the Governor.

(b) IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

Upon the effective date—

  1. The Flux Tax Legislative Oversight Committee shall be formed within 60 days;
  2. The Department of Revenue shall begin planning for Resource Royalty and Consumption Tax infrastructure;
  3. The economic analyses and fiscal modeling required to inform the Legislature’s determination of initial rates shall be commissioned; and
  4. The public education campaign shall begin.

(c) LEGISLATIVE ACTION REQUIRED

No tax shall be collected, no tax shall be repealed, and no Dividend shall be distributed under this Act until the Legislature has enacted the specific rates, thresholds, exemptions, timelines, and implementation details required by each section. This Act creates the framework. The Legislature builds the house.

(d) URGENCY

The Legislature finds that the people of Montana have waited long enough for a tax system that respects their labor, their property, and their intelligence. This Act should be implemented with deliberate speed — carefully enough to get it right, but urgently enough to get it done.
